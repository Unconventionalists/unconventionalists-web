How the brand works

Everything needed to use the Unconventionalists identity consistently: the marks and how to place them, colour, type, voice, the one signature motion, the taglines and where each is used, and the files. Written for us first, and for anyone who makes something with the mark on it.

In one paragraph

Unconventionalists finds overlooked opportunities and builds companies that run themselves. The brand looks like the notebook of a careful experimenter: a hand-drawn rocket, two colours and a cream page, a serif for what we think and a sans for what we do. It is calm rather than loud, dry rather than cold, and it always says where the people are.

2. Using the mark

The rules that keep the mark looking like itself.

Don't

Don't stretch or squash. Scale proportionally only.

Don't recolour. Slate, butter, paper, white or black only.

Don't rotate. The rocket points up. Always.

Don't add effects. No shadows, glows, gradients or outlines.

Don't combine the marks or add a second rocket.

Don't place on busy backgrounds. Give it a flat field of slate or paper.

Unconventionalists

Don't reset the wordmark. It is Libre Caslon Text 400, sentence case.

Don't let the orbits cross the rocket. They pass behind it; use the supplied files.

3. Colour

Five colours, no more. Slate is the brand. Paper is where reading happens. Butter is the single accent and is rationed: the rocket, the ground line, one highlight per screen. Nothing neon, nothing saturated.

4. Type

Two faces. Libre Caslon Text carries anything with a point of view: statements, headlines, the log. Figtree carries anything a person uses rather than reads: navigation, labels, tables, forms. Both are free Google Fonts; both are embedded on the site and must be outlined or embedded in any file that leaves us.

5. Personality and voice

Experimental and lab-like. We write the way a careful person keeps a notebook: dated, specific, a little dry.

Curiousnot scattered: evidence over novelty
Methodicalnot bureaucratic: five stages, one page each
Candidnot provocative: say what happened
Drynot cold: understated humour, no sarcasm
Unconventional in methodnot weird for its own sake

How we write

  • Like a lab notebook: dated, specific, past tense. Say the number if there is one.
  • Name what the agents do; never invoke "AI" as magic. No "revolutionary", "disrupt", "unlock".
  • Always say where the humans are. It's a feature.
  • Sentence case everywhere. No exclamation marks.

Architecture

  • Endorsed: ventures keep their own names and identities.
  • Every venture carries the C1 mark and "an Unconventionalists company".
  • Shutting a venture down is an outcome of the pipeline, published like any other.

Writing: do and don't

Like this

Venture 02 failed validation at the pricing step. 1,840 visitors, 41 sign-ups, no payments. The idea stays in the record with the evidence.

The agents scan 400 sources a week. A person reads the shortlist on Friday and picks one.

Running since March. Nobody has logged in since April.

Not like this

We're thrilled to announce our revolutionary AI-powered platform is disrupting the venture landscape!

Our cutting-edge autonomous agents unlock unprecedented value at scale.

No humans required. Zero headcount. The end of work.

  • Say where the people are. Every description of automation names the human decision that remains. It is the premise, and it is the reassurance.
  • Never frame people as the problem. No "minimal humans", "no humans", "headcount". The joke, when there is one, is about us.
  • Numbers over adjectives. If there is a figure, use it. If there isn't, say what happened.
  • Past tense for the log, present tense for the company. Entries record; pages describe.
  • Sentence case everywhere, including headlines and buttons. No exclamation marks. No emoji.
  • Name the agents' work ("the scan", "the build", "the validation"), never "the AI".
  • Failures are published in the same voice as successes. Shutting a venture is an outcome of the pipeline, not a secret.

8. Taglines

One brand line, one plain description, and three lines with specific jobs. Do not invent new ones for individual pieces; pick from these.

Brand line
Built so we can leave the room.

Site hero, LinkedIn headline, email signature, decks. Usually sits over the description.

Plain description
Companies that run themselves.

The second line under the brand line. Stands alone wherever wit is wrong: legal, investor documents, footers, metadata.

Merch line
Business as unusual.

Stamp, patch, business card, stickers, hoodie if a short line is wanted. Anywhere four words win.

Manifesto line
The future of work is less of it.

Heading of the ventures manifesto page and posters only. Not a signature, not a tote.

Section heading · ventures and log
Every experiment is a company.

Heads the ventures/log section. Earns its keep each time a new company is published.

Body copy
Companies for people with other plans.

A sentence of About-page copy, not a tagline. Use once.
